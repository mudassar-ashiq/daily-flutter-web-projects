Flexbox row

Three cards in a flex row. They share the space evenly, wrap onto a new line when the screen gets narrow, and stay the same height in a row.

justify-content

Controls how the items are spaced along the main axis — for example space-between pushes the first item to the left and the last one to the right.

align-items

Controls how the items line up across the row. The default, stretch, is why these cards are all the same height even though the text is not the same length.

flex-wrap

With wrap turned on, items that do not fit move to the next line instead of squeezing. That is what makes this row work on a phone.

Two-column article

When to use which one

Flexbox is for one direction at a time — a row of buttons, a navigation bar, a set of cards in a line. Grid is for two directions at once — a page made of rows and columns, where an item needs to span across more than one column.

Most pages use both. The header and the card row above are flexbox. The example boxes and the footer below are grid. Neither one replaces the other; they cover different shapes of layout.

What I got wrong at first

I kept setting fixed widths on everything, which broke the layout on small screens. Letting the container decide the width — 1fr in grid, flex: 1 in flexbox — and only adding a media query for the real breakpoints made the page hold together much better.

Quick reference

display: flex
One row or one column of items.
display: grid
Rows and columns together.
gap
Space between items, same in both.
minmax()
Keeps a grid column between a minimum and a maximum width.

Responsive behaviour

One breakpoint at 700px. Below it, the header stacks, the intro becomes one column, the grid drops to a single column, and the sidebar moves under the article.

Screen width Grid columns Sidebar
Over 700px Three columns Beside the article
700px and under One column Under the article
